Gods and goddesses

Afterlife, kingship, fertility

Osiris

Also: Wesir · Asar

Mummiform ruler of the dead, husband of Isis and father of Horus in the familiar Osirian cycle. His restoration after death underlies the identification of the deceased with Osiris. Abydos and Busiris were major centers.

Research references: Egyptian deities — Smarthistory · Regional deities — UCL Digital Egypt

Magic, maternity, restoration

Isis

Also: Aset · Iset

Uses protective knowledge to recover Osiris and safeguard Horus; throne sign and later cow horns mark her images. Her worship spread widely across the Mediterranean.

Research references: Egyptian deities — Smarthistory · Regional deities — UCL Digital Egypt

Sky, kingship

Horus

Also: Heru · Hor

Falcon deity with multiple local and genealogical forms; the living king was identified with Horus, while Horus son of Isis contests Seth for sovereignty.

Research references: Egyptian deities — Smarthistory · Creation traditions — UCL

Storm, desert, conflict, defense

Set

Also: Seth · Sutekh · Setesh

Brother and rival of Osiris in the Ennead; an adversary of Horus but also a defender of the sun against Apep. Worship, iconography and political fortunes changed across regions and dynasties. See the dedicated Set dossier.

Set: ancient cult and mythology

Language

Names and epithets of Seth

Also: Seth · Set · Sutekh · Nubti

Different transliterations reflect Egyptian spellings and later linguistic traditions. Nubti associates him with Nubt. Epithets should be traced to particular monuments rather than treated as one timeless title list.

Research references: Nubt, cult center of Seth — UCL · Regional deities — UCL Digital Egypt

Iconography

The Seth animal

Also: Sha · Set animal

Characteristic curved snout, upright squared ears and stiff or forked tail. No single zoological identification is certain; proposals remain disputed.

Research references: Egyptian deities — Smarthistory · Temple of Seth finds — UCL

Genealogy

Seth in the Ennead

Also: Geb · Nut · Nephthys

In the standard Heliopolitan family Seth belongs to the children of Geb and Nut. The relationships of Isis, Osiris and Nephthys differ in emphasis between narratives.

Research references: Creation traditions — UCL

Myth

Seth and the death of Osiris

Also: Osirian cycle

Seth's violence against Osiris establishes the succession dispute and the conditions for funerary renewal. Surviving Egyptian allusions and later Greek retellings do not tell identical stories.

Research references: Creation traditions — UCL · Divine Egypt — Metropolitan Museum of Art

Myth

Contendings of Horus and Seth

Also: Chester Beatty I

Ramesside literary narrative depicts the gods debating kingship through trials, disputes and competing claims. It preserves humor and ambiguity as well as conflict; it is not the sole version of the myth.

Greco-Roman reception

Also: Plutarch · Greek interpretation

Historical distinction

What an ancient cult of Seth means

Also: Priests · temples · offerings

Cult in Egyptological usage refers to organized veneration of a deity, expressed through sanctuaries, offerings and festivals. There is no established single continuous secret Setian institution spanning antiquity and the modern era.

Research references: Cult and belief — UCL · Nubt, cult center of Seth — UCL · Reinventing religion — Harvard CSWR

Distinction

Seth is not Apep

Also: Chaos · opposition

Seth can threaten order yet also defend it. Apep is a recurring opponent of the sun; their roles cannot be treated as equivalent or as a simple good-versus-evil dualism.

Thelema draws on reinterpreted Egyptian divine names and symbols, including Nuit, Hadit and Ra-Hoor-Khuit. Their modern theological meanings are not equivalent to the ancient cults of Nut, Horus or other gods.

Stele of Revealing

Also: Ankh-ef-en-Khonsu · Crowley

An ancient funerary stele became a central object in Crowley's account of the origins of Thelema. Its original mortuary purpose and modern interpretation are separate historical contexts.

Modern reception

Anton Szandor LaVey

Also: Church of Satan · 1966 · Satanic Bible

LaVey founded the Church of Satan in 1966 and developed a modern Satanist philosophy. Its use of names and imagery from earlier cultures does not demonstrate descent from a pharaonic priesthood.

Research references: The age of Satanism — Oxford Academic · Reinventing religion — Harvard CSWR

Modern reception

Temple of Set

Also: Michael Aquino · 1975 · Setianism

A modern religious organization established in 1975 following a split with LaVey's Church of Satan. Its interpretation of Set belongs to modern esoteric history, not documented survival of the ancient Nubt temple cult.
