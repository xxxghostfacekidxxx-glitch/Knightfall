A letter for Josh

After you knew Josh, you looked at the ground a little differently. Something you’d walked past a hundred times could stop you on the hundred-and-first. Maybe it was a stone with a streak of color through it. You’d turn it over in your hand, and for a minute you could see what all the fuss was about. Josh got so many people collecting rocks and crystals that way. His joy in a thing made you curious enough to look for yourself.

There’s a picture here of Josh down among the stones with a little yellow daffodil beside him. I love that picture. There’s his smile, and there’s this one bright spot of yellow you could so easily walk right by. When I say he helped me find joy in the little things, that’s what I mean. Look at the flower. Pick up the rock. Find somebody you love and show them what you found. That last part was half the fun.

When I was going through depression, I missed being the kid who could get lost in making something just to see how it would turn out. Josh helped me find my way back to that kid. I started wanting to make things again. One of the things I finished was Ash-Fall. I wanted his name here because his friendship is part of the reason this place exists.

Josh was a great father and a fucking great friend. If you needed him, he showed up. I miss that happiness he brought with him. I miss knowing who to tell when some little thing makes my day.

The sea turtle in the Sanctuary carries your nickname, Crush. I wish you could see what you helped me build, Josh. And I wish I could show you the next good rock I find.

— Ronnie
