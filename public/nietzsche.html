1844–1900

Life and intellectual context

A classical philologist before becoming an independent writer, Nietzsche moved from Greek tragedy and Schopenhauer toward an increasingly radical critique of European values. His productive career ended after his collapse in 1889.

Agency

Self-overcoming

Treat one's inherited habits and valuations as material for transformation. Self-overcoming is not merely winning against other people: ask which forms of dependence, complacency or resentment shape one's own conduct.

Modernity

Death of God

The collapse of shared Christian-metaphysical authority creates a problem of justification: inherited values may survive even when their old foundations lose credibility. Read The Gay Science §125 and §343.

Read scholarly reference ↗

Value

Nihilism

When established ends lose their authority, life can appear pointless. Distinguish the diagnosis of this cultural condition from a recommendation to abandon all commitments. Nietzsche explores responses that create or affirm values.

Read scholarly reference ↗

Interpretive dispute

Will to power

A contested account of drives, interpretation and striving. Some readers treat it as psychological, others as broader explanatory philosophy. It should not be reduced to political domination or treated as a settled scientific theory.

Affirmation

Eternal recurrence

The Gay Science §341 asks how one would respond to living the same life again. Scholars debate its cosmological and existential readings. Consider what would make the prospect affirmable.

Affirmation

Amor fati

Love of fate is an aspiration to affirm necessity rather than merely endure it. Compare The Gay Science §276 with Ecce Homo. Acceptance does not require endorsing every harmful act by others.

Read scholarly reference ↗

Zarathustra

Übermensch

The figure of overcoming humanity's existing forms of valuation is a literary and philosophical ideal, not a biological racial category. Read the prologue alongside the work's symbolic narrative.

Knowledge

Perspectivism

Inquiry takes place from particular interests, capacities and positions. This does not automatically make every account equally adequate. Compare Genealogy III §12 and the competing interpretations of Nietzsche's epistemology.

Method

Genealogy

Investigate the history and psychological functions of values rather than assuming they have always meant the same thing. Ask what a value does, who sustains it, and whether its effects are desirable.

Psychology

Ressentiment

In the first essay of Genealogy, reactive hostility plays a role in a proposed history of moral valuation. Distinguish a historical hypothesis from a diagnosis of any particular person.

Read scholarly reference ↗

Ethics

Master and slave morality

Nietzsche contrasts modes of valuation organized around noble self-affirmation and reactive moral reversal. The contrast is controversial; it is not an instruction to enslave, demean or harm others.

Read scholarly reference ↗

Psychology

Bad conscience

The second essay of Genealogy explores guilt, debt, punishment and the inward direction of impulses. Examine the argument and its speculative historical premises.

Read scholarly reference ↗

Religion and culture

Ascetic ideals

The third essay examines how self-denial can give suffering meaning while also constraining life. Nietzsche includes intellectual and artistic forms of asceticism in the discussion.

Read scholarly reference ↗

Ethics

Revaluation of values

Question the authority and effects of existing values and investigate what new valuations might support flourishing. A rejection of one moral scheme is not itself an argument for any replacement.

Aesthetics

Apollonian and Dionysian

The Birth of Tragedy contrasts form, individuation and dream imagery with collective ecstasy and dissolution. The early theory belongs to Nietzsche's evolving account of art, not a timeless taxonomy.

Agency

Free will and responsibility

Nietzsche criticizes moralized accounts of free will and blame. Compare his objections to traditional responsibility with contemporary philosophical alternatives.

Critical reading

Reception and criticism

Read the tensions between individual flourishing, equality, compassion and elitism. Distinguish Nietzsche's published work from posthumous editorial compilations and later political appropriations.

Reading paths and reflection

Begin: Gay Science §§125, 276, 341. Then Zarathustra's prologue. Investigate: Beyond Good and Evil and the three essays of Genealogy. Compare: Schopenhauer on suffering, the Stoics on agency, Kierkegaard on choice and Camus on absurdity.

For each passage record the claim, evidence, strongest objection, and one practical implication. What is the difference between overcoming a habit and controlling someone else? What does affirmation mean when injustice is real? Where might Nietzsche's criticism of equality or compassion fail?
